# Fixed, but open. Why Grade shares on a scalable canvas, not an infinite one.

Every modern design tool eventually hands you an infinite canvas and calls it freedom. Pan forever. Zoom forever. Drop anything anywhere. It feels generous — and then you open someone else's file and spend the first thirty seconds just *finding the work*. The promise of infinite space quietly becomes a tax on attention. The canvas didn't help you think; it gave you somewhere to get lost.

Grade made a different choice. A Grade share is a **fixed but open, scalable canvas with constraints**. Fixed, because the thing you're looking at is *one screen* — bounded, intentional, framed. Open, because you decide how close you stand to it. Scalable, because the frame moves with you: 100% to read the copy, zoom out to take in the whole flow, fit to hold the entire thing in one glance. No void to fall into. No "where did they put it." Just the work, and the dial between immersion and overview.

That dial turns out to matter more than it sounds.

## The problem nobody admits: big screens can't be shared

Real products aren't 1440px. They're trading terminals at 2880, dashboards at 4K, wall displays at 5K. We recently had to prototype at 4K — and discovered that *showing* it was nearly impossible. Traditional tools render at native size and assume everyone has the monitor to match. Most stakeholders don't. So the work that most needs review — the dense, large-canvas product — is the work hardest to put in front of the people who decide its fate.

An infinite canvas doesn't solve this; it just lets you scroll around a thing you still can't see whole. **Scale-to-fit does.** Because a Grade screen is a fixed artboard the viewer can shrink to fit their glass, a 4K prototype becomes legible on a laptop — the entire composition, at once, then zoomable to pixel detail where it counts. That's not a feature of an infinite canvas. It's a feature of a *bounded* one.

## Constraints are the point, not the compromise

We give the viewer real controls — zoom, light/dark, theme treatments, soon device presets and comments — but every one of them operates *inside the frame*. You can't wander off. You can't accidentally nudge the artwork. You get as much immersion or as much detail as you need, and nothing you didn't ask for.

This is deliberate. Design *work* is messy: dead ends, alternates, half-built states, the argument you had with yourself at 2am. That mess belongs in the studio. A **share** is a finished thought. So we let you bring the *thinking* — alternate themes, versions, annotations, the states a component can be in — without bringing the chaos. The viewer sees a calm, single screen, and can choose to explode it into its variations only when they want to.

> Distraction-free flows and views — don't get lost on the promise of the infinite canvas.

An infinite canvas optimises for the author mid-flight. A fixed, scalable canvas optimises for the moment that actually decides whether the work ships: someone else, on their own screen, seeing it clearly. Grade is built for that moment.
